How to Calculate Concrete Bags

To estimate concrete bags, first calculate the total concrete volume in cubic feet.

Formula: length × width × thickness in feet

Because thickness is usually measured in inches, divide the thickness by 12 before multiplying. Then divide the total cubic feet by the approximate yield of your selected bag size.

Example: A 10 ft × 10 ft slab at 4 inches thick is about 33.33 cubic feet. With 80 lb bags that yield about 0.60 cubic feet each, you would need about 56 bags before adding extra for waste.

Should You Buy Extra Concrete?

Yes. It is usually smart to buy 5% to 10% extra concrete for waste, spillage, form irregularities, uneven ground, and small measurement differences. Running short during a pour can create delays and uneven results.

Concrete Bag Calculator FAQ

How many bags of concrete do I need?

Enter your project length, width, thickness, and bag size. The calculator estimates the total concrete volume and rounds up to the nearest full bag.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete do I need?

An 80 lb bag usually yields about 0.60 cubic feet. Divide your total cubic feet by 0.60 and round up. For example, a 10 ft × 10 ft slab at 4 inches thick needs about 56 bags of 80 lb concrete before adding extra for waste.

How many 60 lb bags of concrete do I need?

A 60 lb bag usually yields about 0.45 cubic feet. Divide your total cubic feet by 0.45 and round up to estimate the number of 60 lb bags needed.

How many bags of concrete are in one cubic yard?

One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet. That is about 45 bags of 80 lb concrete, 60 bags of 60 lb concrete, or 72 bags of 50 lb concrete, depending on the exact product yield.

What slab thickness is common?

Many residential slabs are 4 inches thick, but some projects require thicker pours. Always follow your project plans, local requirements, and product instructions.

Can I use this calculator for footings, posts, and pads?

Yes. This calculator works well for slabs, shed pads, rectangular footings, post bases, fence footings, and similar small concrete projects.

Should I round up when buying concrete bags?

Yes. Always round up and consider buying 5% to 10% extra so you do not run short during the pour.
